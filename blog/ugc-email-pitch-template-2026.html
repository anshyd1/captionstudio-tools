Quick answer: the UGC pitch beginners can actually send

A good UGC pitch is short, specific, and useful. It should show that you understand the brand, suggest a clear content idea, link your portfolio, and ask for one simple next step.

Copy-paste UGC email pitch

Subject: UGC idea for [Brand Name]

Hi [Name],

I’m [Your Name], a UGC creator who creates short-form videos for [niche/category] brands.

I came across [Brand Name] and had a simple content idea: a [video angle] showing how [product/service] helps [specific customer problem]. I think this could work well as an organic Reel or short ad-style creative.

You can see my portfolio here: [Portfolio Link]

Would you be open to me sending over 2–3 UGC concepts for your next campaign?

Best,
[Your Name]

Fast pitch rules
  • Keep it short. A brand manager should understand your point in under 20 seconds.
  • Make it specific. Mention a real product, campaign, content angle, or customer problem.
  • Show one idea. Do not send a long essay with five unrelated services.
  • Add portfolio link. Make it easy for the brand to check your style.
  • Ask one question. “Would you be open to…” is cleaner than pushing for a deal immediately.

Visual examples: what UGC outreach is built around

A strong pitch is not just writing. It is the combination of a clear content idea, a simple portfolio, a brand-relevant angle, and confident communication.

What brands actually want in a UGC pitch

Brands do not need another creator saying “I love your products.” They need a reason to believe you can create content that feels natural, matches their audience, and helps them test useful creative angles.

A strong pitch does not beg for a chance. It shows the brand a useful angle they may not have tried yet.

Brands usually look for five things

  • Relevance: Your niche, style, or audience understanding fits the brand.
  • Clarity: You explain what type of content you can create.
  • Proof: Your portfolio shows examples, even if they are practice examples.
  • Professionalism: Your message is clean, polite, and easy to reply to.
  • Usable ideas: Your pitch suggests content that could become a Reel, ad, Story, or product demo.

If you need better content angles for your pitch, use Instagram content ideas for small businesses. Many of those examples can become UGC pitch concepts for product demos, customer problem videos, or behind-the-scenes brand content.

The best UGC pitch email structure

A pitch should be easy to scan. Do not overload the first email with your life story, every package, and a long rate card. Start with relevance and one idea.

Part What to write Example
Subject line Short, clear, brand-specific. UGC idea for your skincare launch
Opening Introduce yourself in one line. I’m a UGC creator who creates short-form videos for wellness brands.
Brand reason Mention why you are contacting them. I noticed your new product line and had a simple demo idea.
Content angle Give one useful idea. A “first use reaction” video showing the customer problem and payoff.
Portfolio Add one clean link. Portfolio: [link]
CTA Ask for a low-pressure next step. Would you be open to seeing 2–3 concepts?
Beginner tip

If your pitch is longer than your portfolio page, it is probably too long. Keep the first message clean and let the brand ask for more details.

Instagram DM pitch templates for UGC creators

Instagram DMs should be even shorter than email. Do not send five paragraphs in the first message. Start with a simple opener and move the conversation to email if needed.

DM template: soft opener

Hi [Brand Name] team! I’m a UGC creator and had a short-form video idea for [specific product]. Is there an email where I can send a quick concept and portfolio?

DM template: portfolio mention

Hey! I create UGC-style videos for [niche] brands. I think [product/service] could work well with a [specific angle] video. Can I send my portfolio and 2 content ideas?

DM template: after brand replies

Amazing, thank you! My portfolio is here: [Portfolio Link]. I can create UGC around [angle 1], [angle 2], or [angle 3]. If helpful, I can send a short concept list by email too.

UGC follow-up email templates

Follow-ups work best when they are polite and useful. Do not guilt-trip the brand. Add a new idea, keep it short, and make it easy to reply.

Follow-up 1: gentle reminder

Subject: Re: UGC idea for [Brand Name]

Hi [Name],

Just following up on my note below. I had a UGC concept for [specific product/campaign] and would be happy to send over a few short-form video angles if you are currently exploring creator content.

Portfolio: [Portfolio Link]

Best,
[Your Name]

Follow-up 2: new angle

Subject: Another UGC angle for [Brand Name]

Hi [Name],

One more quick idea: a [video style] showing [customer problem] and how [product/service] helps solve it. This could be useful for organic social or as a simple creative test.

If UGC is not a priority right now, no worries at all.

Best,
[Your Name]

Follow-up rule

Send one or two follow-ups, then move on. A clean creator workflow means pitching consistently without becoming pushy.

Pricing lines to use in a UGC pitch

You do not always need to include your full rate in the first pitch. But you should know how to talk about pricing when the brand asks.

Pricing line: rate card available

I’d be happy to send my rate card once I know the deliverables and usage rights you need. My pricing depends on video length, number of concepts, usage, revisions, and delivery timeline.

Pricing line: package option

For this type of campaign, I usually recommend a 3-video starter package so the brand can test multiple hooks and angles instead of relying on one concept.

Pricing line: usage rights

Before I quote, can you confirm whether the content is for organic posting only or paid ad usage? Usage rights affect the final rate.

UGC pitch checklist before you send

Before sending a pitch, check whether it feels like a real message to a real brand — not a mass copy-paste blast.

Before sending, confirm:
  • You used the correct brand name.
  • You mentioned a real product, service, campaign, or audience.
  • Your content idea is clear in one sentence.
  • Your portfolio link works.
  • Your message is short enough to skim.
  • You did not promise guaranteed sales, viral results, or unrealistic outcomes.
  • You asked one simple next-step question.

UGC pitch mistakes beginners make

Most beginner pitches fail because they are too vague. The brand cannot tell what the creator offers, why it matters, or what the next step should be.

Mistake Why it hurts Better move
“I love your brand” with no idea Feels generic and easy to ignore. Add one specific content angle.
Long first email Busy brand teams may not read it. Keep it short and link your portfolio.
No portfolio link The brand cannot check your style quickly. Add one clean portfolio link.
Fake performance claims Damages trust and sounds spammy. Talk about concepts, not guarantees.
Quoting before scope You may undercharge for usage or revisions. Ask about deliverables and usage first.

FAQs about UGC email pitch templates

Write a short introduction, mention the brand or product naturally, suggest one content idea, add your portfolio link, and ask whether they are open to UGC concepts or collaborations.

Yes, but they should build a simple portfolio first. Practice videos, product demos, testimonials, and problem-solution clips can help brands understand your style even before your first paid deal.

You can say your rate card is available, but it is often better to first confirm deliverables, usage rights, timeline, and whether the brand is open to UGC content.

Email is usually better for detailed pitches, portfolios, and pricing. Instagram DM can work as a short opener to ask for the right email contact.

Keep the first pitch short — usually around 5 to 8 concise lines. The goal is to earn a reply, not explain every package in the first message.

Conclusion: send a useful pitch, not a desperate one

A good UGC pitch does not need to be complicated. It needs to be specific, useful, and easy to reply to. Mention the brand, suggest one idea, share your portfolio, and ask for a simple next step.

If you are nervous, start with a small list of brands you genuinely understand. Send thoughtful pitches, track replies, improve your portfolio, and keep your follow-ups polite.
